1 Baseline Payment Parameters and Archetypal Spending Assumptions

To accurately estimate the structural flow of the systemic subsidy, we must first establish the baseline of incoming state fiat and explicitly define the behavioral spending assumptions forced upon each specific recipient archetype. The welfare recipient does not operate within a free market of rational choice; their spending habits are dictated entirely by severe financial scarcity and the biological necessity of survival. As of the March 2026 Services Australia indexation adjustments (Services Australia), the maximum fortnightly payments are capped into distinct brackets. Because individuals subsisting on these incomes live severely below the recognized poverty line, their marginal propensity to consume is estimated at near 100 percent. There is zero capacity for wealth accumulation. We base the estimated corporate flow-through on the following structural assumptions for each demographic:

Demographic Scale and Population Baselines

To accurately project these individual financial extractions to a national macroeconomic scale in the later sections of this matrix, we must establish the precise population sizes of these archetypes. According to 2025/2026 demographic data published by the Department of Social Services (DSS) and the Australian Institute of Health and Welfare (AIHW):

  • Youth Allowance (YA) Cohort: Comprises approximately 240,000 individuals.
  • JobSeeker Payment (JSP) Cohort: Comprises approximately 875,000 individuals.
  • Age Pension & Disability Support Pension (DSP) Cohort: Comprises approximately 3,520,000 individuals (~2.7 million Age Pensioners and ~820,000 DSP recipients).
Model A

Youth Allowance (YA)

Base Rate: $677.20 / fn

Assumes forced hyper-dense shared housing splitting rent/utilities across 3-5 people. Spending skewed toward digital connectivity and public transport.

The "Co-Dependent Fractional" Split: It is mathematically impossible for a YA recipient to survive independently in the 2026 private rental market. This forces hyper-dense shared housing arrangements, splitting utility bills and rent across 3 to 5 people.

Their spending habits are highly skewed towards digital connectivity (due to study/apprentice needs) and public transport. Because their base fiat is so low, their food budget is dedicated almost entirely to high-volume, low-cost carbohydrates, pushing a higher percentage of grocery spend toward discount retailers like ALDI.

Their financial deficit is bridged either by the gig economy or by unrecorded parental micro-subsidies.

Model B

JobSeeker (JSP)

Base Rate: $808.70 / fn

Locked out of public housing and private rentals (rent consuming 65-75% of payment). Food is elastic, resulting in starvation to absorb bills.

The "Survival Squeeze": Representing the most severe poverty-induced structural stress, the JSP recipient is locked out of public housing and private accommodation, with rent consuming 65% to 75% of income.

Because rent and utilities are rigid, food is their only elastic variable. The JSP recipient routinely engages in enforced starvation (skipping meals) to absorb utility bill shocks.

Lacking fuel money, they are geographically trapped to shop at whatever supermarket duopoly is closest, ensuring guaranteed capture by Woolworths or Coles. Transport spending is hoarded strictly to meet punitive mutual obligations.

Model C

Pensioner / DSP

Base Rate: $1,200.90 / fn

Higher base consumed by severe physiological/medical overheads. High utility spend due to climate control/medical equipment needs.

The "Inelastic Overhead": Though receiving a higher base, this surplus is immediately consumed by inescapable biological and medical overheads.

Utility spend is aggressively higher due to continuous climate control (preventing heatstroke/hypothermia) or powered medical equipment. Out-of-pocket health costs represent a massive structural flow-through straight to pharmacies.

Groceries are rigid, requiring specific dietary purchases that prevent relying on cheap bulk carbohydrates, locking spend into higher-margin major supermarket aisles.

The Billing Cycle & Cash Flow Mismatch

A critical friction point in low-income budgeting is the structural mismatch between fortnightly Services Australia payment cycles and telecommunications billing cycles:

1. 28-Day Prepaid SIMs

Strict 28-day cycles rather than calendar months. A standard $30 recharge every 28 days translates to a cash flow allocation of ~$15.00 per fortnight ($30 / 2).

2. Monthly NBN / Sharehouse

Standard monthly NBN bills ($60-$80/month) convert to $27.70 to $36.90 per fortnight. Split across 3-4 sharehouse occupants, this reduces to $7.00 to $12.00 per fortnight per person.

3. Annual Long-Expiry SIMs

Low-income recipients frequently utilize 365-day prepaid SIMs ($150-$200 upfront). A $180 annual plan represents an amortized cost of $6.92 per fortnight.

5 The Pharmaceutical and Health Extraction

Statutory PBS Safety Net

  • PBS Prescription Copay: Strictly capped at $7.70 per script for concessional cardholders.
  • Annual Safety Net Threshold: Capped at 36 scripts ($277.20 annually or ~$10.65 per fortnight). Subsequent scripts are free.

Out-of-Pocket Spending

  • Seniors Spend Survey: Average of $1,586 annually in out-of-pocket health expenses (non-PBS over-the-counter medicine, vitamins, allied health gap fees).

Chemist Warehouse / Sigma Macro Capture

9 Treasury Alignment and Demographic Taper

To align the matrix with actual Treasury expenditure, we apply a demographic taper. Real-world data indicates that approximately 32% to 33% of Age Pensioners are part-pensioners (income/asset tested), and roughly 14% of DSP recipients receive part-payments.

Factoring in partnered rate reductions (applied to 20-25% of recipients), the net national fortnightly welfare injection is calculated at ~$3.19 Billion. This aligns with our findings, confirming that core oligopolies capture approximately 68% to 75% of direct cash distributed by Services Australia.

10 Federal Budget & Outlay Validation

We stress-test this extraction matrix against empirical administrative files published by DSS and the AIHW:

Total Recipient Volume: Approximately 5.3 million Australians receive a continuous state income payment (Age Pension: ~2.60M, JSP: ~821k-875k, DSP: ~823k, YA: ~241.5k, Parent/Carer: ~800k). Our primary focus on YA, JSP, Age Pension, and DSP captures 87% of the total direct cash volume distributed by Services Australia.

Direct Cash outlays: The Gross Federal Budget allocates $290.9B to "Social Security and Welfare". Stripping away institutional non-cash programs (NDIS at ~$50B+, aged care, childcare subsidies) leaves direct cash transfers at $125 Billion to $132 Billion annually (~$4.8B-$5.1B/fn gross). After partnered reductions and tapers, net fortnightly cash deposited directly into recipient bank accounts is confirmed at $3.25 Billion to $3.50 Billion.

11 Analysis of the Macro Discrepancy & Uncaptured Residual Fiat

Tracking the uncaptured residual variance of $530 Million to $1.31 Billion per fortnight reveals how the remainder of state welfare currency is absorbed by secondary corporate and financial mechanisms:

A. Vehicle & Transport ($200M - $350M / fn)

AAA Transport Index confirms average fuel spend of $189.90/fn. Captured by fuel retailers (Ampol, Shell/Viva, BP, 7-Eleven) and the insurance oligopoly (IAG, Suncorp, QBE) along with state transit registration fees.

B. Non-Rent Housing ($180M - $300M / fn)

Roughly 70% of Age Pensioners own their homes outright. Their uncaptured fiat is directed into municipal council rates, water access charges, strata body corporate fees, and trade maintenance services.

C. BNPL & High-Interest Debt ($80M - $150M / fn)

ASIC and Finder stats show 33% of BNPL (Afterpay, Zip) users use credit for groceries. Late fees, card interest, and account keeping fees represent high-margin non-bank financial extractions.

D. Secondary Retail ($70M - $150M / fn)

Wesfarmers' Kmart Group (generating over $11.4 Billion in annual revenue) targets value-conscious low-income shoppers, along with Big W, second-hand charities, and local micro-services.

E. State Tax & Excise Recapture ($50M - $100M / fn)

Federal fuel excise (49.6c/L), tobacco/alcohol excises, and the standard GST act as a fiscal sponge, draining a predictable fraction of issued fiat directly back to the federal treasury.
